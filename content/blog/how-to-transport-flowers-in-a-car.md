Collecting flowers is usually the easy part. The drive is where stems get bent, water spills and petals soften in the heat. Knowing how to transport flowers in a car comes down to three things: stopping movement, keeping cut stems supplied with water and avoiding heat. The details depend on whether you are carrying a wrapped bouquet, loose stems in a bucket or a finished vase arrangement. If you are still deciding what to give, [The art of a thoughtful bouquet](/blog/choosing-a-thoughtful-bouquet) can help you choose something that suits the person and is practical to carry.

## Choose a safe spot in the car

The best place is flat, out of direct sun and away from the driver. A passenger or rear footwell, or a flat boot floor, usually works well. Never put flowers, a vase or a box in the driver's footwell or anywhere they could slide under the pedals.

## How to transport flowers in a car when they are wrapped

- Stand the bouquet upright in a sturdy bag or box, wedged with a folded towel so it cannot tip.
- If it must lie flat, place it on a seat with the heads towards the middle of the car, clear of the door.
- Keep it out of direct sun and turn vents so air does not blow straight onto delicate petals.
- Drive as directly as practical and never leave flowers in a parked car.

Warmth matters more than many people expect. [Oklahoma State University Extension](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/the-care-and-handling-of-cut-flowers) explains that higher temperatures increase respiration and water loss in cut flowers, which can lead to wilting.

## For flowers in a vase: stabilise the container first

A vase is most likely to spill when it can slide, so secure it before you start the engine.

1. Place it on a level floor area rather than a seat.
2. Set the vase inside a box or crate that fits closely around its base.
3. Pack clean towels or crumpled paper around the vase to stop it shifting.
4. If the vase is very full, pour off some water, leaving the cut stem ends well covered.

Keep the container upright throughout. Do not hold an arrangement while driving, and do not ask a passenger to balance it by hand; a sudden stop can still tip it.

## For loose stems in a bucket

Unarranged stems can knock against each other and the rim even when the bucket is steady.

- Stand the bucket in a snug crate or box, or wedge it with towels.
- Keep the stems gathered, loosely tied or in their sleeve so they cannot splay.
- Use just enough water to cover the cut ends.
- Make sure tall stems are not resting against the roof or doors.

## Keep flowers away from heat, cold and exhaust

Heater vents and sunny rear shelves warm up quickly. In cold weather, keep the wrapping on for the walk between the shop and the car.

If you have groceries with you, keep ripening fruit away from the flowers. [NC State Extension’s postharvest guidance](https://cutflowers.ces.ncsu.edu/cutflowers-postharvest/) notes that ethylene from ripening fruit and from engine exhaust can damage susceptible cut flowers. Avoid waiting with flowers beside an idling vehicle.

## What to do as soon as you arrive

Put the stems into clean water promptly and check for bent stems or bruised petals. [University of Florida IFAS](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/design/outdoor-living/cut-flower-care/) recommends a clean vase and a fresh cut with a clean, sharp tool. For the fuller routine over the following days, see [A little care, a little longer](/blog/flowers-that-last).

## A quick checklist before you set off

- Do I have a flat, protected spot away from the driver's pedals?
- Will the flowers stay upright and clear of doors, seat edges and direct sun?
- Do I have a box, bucket or towels if the flowers are travelling in water?
